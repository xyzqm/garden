I like to think of CS as an expansion pack for math that adds one additional dimension to perfect: **efficiency**.[^1] For instance, [[Hall's marriage theorem]] beautifully characterizes which bipartite graphs admit perfect matchings, but tells you nothing about how to actually *find* that perfect matching efficiently. [Euler's criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler%27s_criterion) tells you when $sqrt(a)$ is defined under a certain mod $p$, but nothing about how to actually find $sqrt(a)$. Thus, CS is fundamentally about solving mathematical problems efficiently. 

[^1]: You may want to read [[A love letter to math|my love letter to math]] first, where I elaborate further on perfection.
